1. the inherited equality between inverse support and reduced root span;

This note closes those interfaces in the complex polynomial setting.  The

## 2. The inherited root-span interface

Let `P` be monic and centred of degree `d >= 2`.  There is a unique series

\[
\xi(t)=t+O(t^{-1})\in t\,\mathbf C[[t^{-1}]]
\quad\text{with}\quad P(\xi(t))=t^d.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Write its residue decomposition

\[
X_r(t)=\frac1d\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\zeta_d^{-jr}
             \xi(\zeta_d^jt),
\qquad
\Sigma(P)=\{r\bmod d:X_r\ne0\}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The inverse branches at infinity are

\[
x_j=\xi(\zeta_d^jt)
    =\sum_{r\bmod d}\zeta_d^{jr}X_r.
\tag{2.3}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 — Root span equals inverse support

For every complex polynomial `P`,

\[
\boxed{\dim_{\mathbf C}W_P=|\Sigma(P)|.}
\tag{2.4}
\]

### Proof

The matrices in (2.2) and (2.3) are inverse discrete Fourier matrices, so the
constant span of the branches `x_j` equals the constant span of the channels
`X_r`.  Distinct nonzero channels are linearly independent over `C`: every
Laurent exponent in `X_r` is congruent to `r` modulo `d`, and different
residue classes are disjoint.  Centring gives `X_0=0`, which is precisely the
removed trace channel.  This proves (2.4).  The computation takes place in
`C((t^{-1}))`; passing to the algebraic function field generated by the roots
does not change a constant-linear dimension.  \(\square\)

The formal-moment isomorphism already proved in `MANUSCRIPT.md`, Theorems
4.3 and 5.2, identifies the active seed channels with the same set `Sigma(P)`.
Consequently

\[
\boxed{q(P)=|\Sigma(P)|=\dim_{\mathbf C}W_P.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

No analytic contour comparison is used in (2.4) or (2.5).

### Corollary 2.2 — Right-factor projection

If `P=R circ Q`, `m=deg Q`, and `d=m n`, then

\[
q(Q)=|\Sigma(P)\bmod m|.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Indeed, Fourier inversion within a `Q`-block groups the channels according to
their residues modulo `m`.  Channels with different residues have disjoint
Laurent supports.  This also proves heredity `q(Q)<=q(P)`.  Equality holds if
and only if the projection `Sigma(P) -> Z/mZ` is injective; equivalently every
generic `Q`-block spans all of `W_P`.  We call this **full fusion**.

This is the only root-span input used below.

## 3. Finite-pole and infinity-order descent

Let `A(Y)` be monic of degree `n`, let `y_1,...,y_n` be the roots of
`A(Y)-z`, and choose elements `v_i` such that

\[
v_i^m=y_i-b.
\tag{3.1}
\]

After the scaling lemma, monodromy acts by

\[
(v_1,\ldots,v_n)\longmapsto
\epsilon_\gamma(v_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(n)}),
\qquad \epsilon_\gamma^m=1.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Put `f_k=e_k(v_1,...,v_n)`.

### Lemma 3.1 — Invariant-integral descent

For every `1 <= k <= n`, `f_k^m` belongs to `C[z]`.  Its pole order at
infinity is at most `k/n`.

### Proof

Equation (3.2) sends `f_k` to `epsilon_gamma^k f_k`, so `f_k^m` is fixed by
the full geometric monodromy and therefore lies in the rational base field
`C(z)`.

Each `y_i` is integral over `C[z]`, being a root of the monic polynomial
`A(Y)-z`.  Each `v_i` is integral over `C[z,y_i]` by (3.1).  Transitivity of
integrality makes every `f_k`, and hence `f_k^m`, integral over `C[z]`.
Because `C[z]` is integrally closed in `C(z)`,

\[
f_k^m\in C(z)\cap\overline{C[z]}=C[z].
\tag{3.3}
\]

At a place above infinity, `y_i=O(z^{1/n})` and
`v_i=O(z^{1/(mn)})`.  Every summand of `f_k` is a product of `k` such
terms, so

\[
f_k^m=O(z^{k/n}).
\tag{3.4}
\]

This is the asserted infinity bound.  \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 — Constancy below the top coefficient

If `k<n`, then `f_k^m` is a polynomial of integer degree at most `k/n<1`.
It is therefore constant.  Since `C` is algebraically closed and the function
field is an integral domain, `f_k` itself is constant.

For `k=n`, there is an exact identity, including its leading coefficient:

\[
f_n^m=\prod_i(y_i-b)=(-1)^n(A(b)-z).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus the final coefficient is affine and nonconstant.  The varying radical
polynomial consequently has all lower coefficients constant and only its
constant term varying.  This justifies the input `B(t)+e t^n` used in the
uniform coefficient extraction, without an analytic boundedness assumption.

## 4. A stable line and the first rank increase

### Lemma 4.1 — Stable line forces a power

If `W_P` contains a nonzero monodromy-stable line, then `P` is affine-
equivalent to a power.

### Proof

Let the line carry a character `chi`.  At a chosen point over infinity, only
the leading inertia character can have a pole.  A stable line in any other
character would have no pole over infinity and, because roots are integral
over `C[z]`, no finite pole; it would be constant, impossible in the reduced
span.  Hence `chi(tau)=zeta_d` for the full `d`-cycle `tau` at infinity.

If `H` is a root stabiliser, the projection of that root to the line is
nonzero and fixed by `H`, so `H<=ker chi`.  Now `[G:H]=d`, while the image of
`chi` contains an element of order `d`.  Hence `H=ker chi`.  The root action
is faithful, so the core of `H` is trivial; normality gives `H=1` and
`G=C_d`.  A complex polynomial with cyclic geometric monodromy is affine-
equivalent to `x^d`.  \(\square\)

Therefore every nonpower root-span representation of dimension two or three
is irreducible.

Choose a complete decomposition and the maximal power suffix `x^m+b`.  Let
`A` be the next indecomposable factor.  Its generic roots determine distinct
projective radical lines spanning a space of dimension at most three.  The
monodromy of `A` is primitive and contains a full cycle.

For completeness, the radical lines are distinct because a proportionality
`u_i=lambda u_j` would imply
`y_i-b=lambda^m(y_j-b)`.  Since a generic root is transcendental over `C`,
this is the polynomial identity
`A(lambda^m(Y-b)+b)=A(Y)`.  A nonidentity affine deck symmetry gives a
nontrivial quotient decomposition of `A`; the remaining identity case would
make two roots equal.  The maximal-power choice excludes the sole
indecomposable affine-deck case.

## 5. Source-exact primitive-factor audit

The audit uses two complete imported lists.

1. Theorem 3.3 of Behajaina--Koenig--Neftin, *Monodromy groups of
   polynomials of composition length 2*, arXiv:2603.27609v2, states that an
   indecomposable complex polynomial has monodromy of one of the following
   types: prime cyclic or dihedral; degree-four `S_4`; natural `S_n` or odd
   natural `A_n`; or one of the explicitly listed almost-simple groups in
   degrees `6,7,8,9,10,11,13,15,21,23,31`.
2. The finite primitive subgroups of `PGL_2(C)` are `A_4,S_4,A_5`; the
   primitive subgroups of `PGL_3(C)` are
   `A_5, PSL_2(7), A_6, H_1, H_2, H_3`.  The latter table is Table 2 of
   Calbeto, *On the finite irreducible subgroups of GL_n(C) and PGL_n(C)*,
   arXiv:2510.00718v1.

The older source `Burda--Khovanskii`, arXiv:1209.5137, gives the same
Muller list in its theorem labelled `mullerthm`; it is retained as an
independent source-to-list comparison.

### Proposition 5.1 — Low-dimensional primitive audit

If the distinct radical lines belonging to the indecomposable factor `A`
span dimension at most three, then `A` is affine-equivalent to

1. a prime power;
2. a prime Dickson/Chebyshev polynomial; or
3. a quartic with monodromy `S_4`.

### Proof

In dimension two, three distinct projective points make the projective action
faithful.  Intersecting the `PGL_2` list with the polynomial-monodromy list
leaves the cyclic and dihedral families and the formal candidates `S_4` in
degree four and `A_5` in degree five.  Their point stabilisers are `S_3` and
`A_4`.  A finite subgroup of `PGL_2(C)` fixing a point is cyclic, so neither
candidate can stabilise a radical line.  The solvable cases are precisely
prime powers, prime Dickson polynomials, and degree four.

In dimension three, an irreducible imprimitive finite linear group is
monomial: its diagonal subgroup is abelian and its quotient permutes three
one-dimensional imprimitivity spaces through `S_3`.  It is therefore
solvable.  A nonsolvable candidate must consequently act primitively.  The
radical lines are distinct (proportional radicals would give a constant affine
relation between two distinct generic roots and hence a nontrivial block for
the primitive root action), so the induced permutation action on those lines
is the faithful root action.  Thus every nonsolvable candidate embeds
faithfully in `PGL_3(C)` and belongs to the primitive table quoted above.

The source-exact intersection of the two lists is now only

\[
A_5\text{ in degree }5,
\qquad
PSL_3(2)\simeq PSL_2(7)\text{ in degree }7.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For the three-dimensional `A_5` character and its point stabiliser `A_4`,

\[
\langle\chi|_{A_4},1\rangle
=\frac{3+3(-1)+8(0)}{12}=0.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The two nontrivial linear characters of `A_4` give the same zero because the
three-cycle character value is zero.  Hence `A_4` fixes no projective line.

For the Klein three-dimensional representation of `PSL_3(2)` and point
stabiliser `S_4`, the character values on identity, order two, order three,
and order four are `3,-1,0,1`.  Therefore

\[
\langle\chi|_{S_4},1\rangle
=\frac{3+9(-1)+8(0)+6(1)}{24}=0.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Multiplication by the sign character also gives zero:

\[
\frac{3+6-3-6}{24}=0.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus `S_4` fixes no projective line.  Both exceptional candidates are
excluded, proving the proposition.  \(\square\)

This expands every group, degree, stabiliser, and character used by the old
one-paragraph audit.  No unspecified "finite exceptional list" remains.

## 6. The full-block kernel lemma

Let `P=R circ Q`, and let `V_1,...,V_N` be the spans of the generic
`Q`-blocks, where `N=deg R`.

### Lemma 6.1 — A fixed full-span block detects the identity

Assume `q(P)=q(Q)`.  Then `V_i=W_P` for every `i`.  If an element of the
block kernel fixes one `Q`-block pointwise, it is the identity.

### Proof

Heredity gives `dim V_i=q(Q)=q(P)=dim W_P`, and `V_i<=W_P`; hence
`V_i=W_P`.  An element fixing the roots in one block fixes their span, so it
acts identically on `W_P`.  Every inverse root of `P` belongs to `W_P`; the
element fixes all roots.  The root action is faithful.  \(\square\)

### Corollary 6.2 — Partial-support kernels are impossible

Inside the wreath-product embedding, every coordinate projection of the
block kernel is injective.  In particular, no nonidentity kernel element can
be supported on a proper subset of the outer blocks.

This is the missing implication between full fusion and monodromy rigidity.
It is stronger than the invalid shortcut "two tetrahedra span the same space,
therefore they are the same tetrahedron".

### Lemma 6.3 — The full block stabiliser induces the absolute block monodromy

Let `M/C(t)` be the splitting field of `P(X)-t`, let `z` be one root of
`R(Y)-t`, and let `B` be the corresponding block of roots of `Q(X)-z`.
Then the setwise stabiliser `S_B` induces the full geometric monodromy group
of `Q` on `B`.

### Proof

The block system is the one attached to the intermediate field `C(t,z)`.
Consequently

\[
S_B=\operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbf C(t,z)).
\]

Let `L_B` be the splitting field of `Q(X)-z` over `C(z)`.  It is an
intermediate Galois field of `M/C(t,z)`.  Restriction in a finite Galois
extension is surjective onto the Galois group of every Galois intermediate
field, so

\[
S_B\twoheadrightarrow
\operatorname{Gal}(L_B/\mathbf C(z))=\operatorname{Mon}(Q).
\]

If `g` carries `B` to another block `B'`, then
`gS_Bg^{-1}=S_{B'}`.  Under rank preservation, both block spans equal
`W_P`; hence the linear action of `g` on `W_P` conjugates the two full induced
block-monodromy representations.  This is the full-group conjugacy used by
the normaliser arguments below, rather than a claim that a coordinate image
of the outer block kernel is automatically the absolute monodromy.
\(\square\)

## 7. Rank-preserving extensions in ranks one and two

### Rank one

Lemma 4.1 gives `Q=x^m`; applying it to `P` gives

\[
P=x^{mn},\qquad R=y^n.
\tag{7.1}
\]

### Dickson rank two

Uniformise a generic `D_m(x,a)` fibre by

\[
x=U+a/U,
\qquad W_Q=\langle U,U^{-1}\rangle.
\tag{7.2}
\]

If a second fibre with parameter `V` has the same plane, write

\[
V=\alpha U+\beta U^{-1},
\qquad
V^{-1}=\gamma U+\delta U^{-1}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The identity `VV^{-1}=1` and nonconstancy of `U` give

\[
\alpha\gamma=\beta\delta=0.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Invertibility leaves `V=cU` or `V=cU^{-1}`.  We now identify the entire
extension, without a prime-factor or adjacency assumption.

Let `M/C(t)` be the splitting field of `P(X)-t`.  Writing the roots in the
reference `Q`-block as

\[
x_j=\zeta_m^jU+a\zeta_m^{-j}U^{-1},
\]

the two Fourier projections (valid for `m>=3`) are

\[
U=\frac1m\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\zeta_m^{-j}x_j,
\qquad
\frac aU=\frac1m\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\zeta_m^jx_j.
\]

Thus the reference block recovers `U` and `a/U`, so `C(U)<=M`.  Rank
preservation makes every block span equal to
`<U,U^{-1}>`.  Applying (7.3)--(7.4) to a uniformizer of every other block
shows that every inverse root of `P` lies in `C(U)`.  Hence

\[
M=\mathbf C(U).
\tag{7.5}
\]

Every element of `Gamma=Gal(C(U)/C(t))` therefore sends
`U` to `cU` or `c/U`.  Thus `Gamma` is a finite subgroup of the normalizer of
the multiplicative torus in `PGL_2(C)`.  It contains the absolute block group

\[
D_m=\langle U\mapsto\zeta_mU,\ U\mapsto a/U\rangle.
\]

The rotational subgroup of `Gamma` is finite cyclic, say `mu_N`, and the
displayed reflection gives `|Gamma|=2N`.  On the other hand,

\[
[\mathbf C(U):\mathbf C(t)]
=2\deg P=2m\deg R=2mn.
\]

Thus `N=mn` (and in particular `mu_m<=mu_N`), and

\[
\Gamma=\langle U\mapsto\zeta_NU,\ U\mapsto a/U\rangle\cong D_N.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Its invariant field is

\[
\mathbf C(U)^\Gamma
=\mathbf C\left(U^N+\frac{a^N}{U^N}\right)
=\mathbf C(D_N(x,a)).
\tag{7.7}
\]

Since this field is `C(t)=C(P(x))`, the two generators differ by a Mobius
transformation.  Both are polynomials in `x` with their sole pole at infinity,
so that transformation is affine.  Consequently `P=A circ D_N(x,a)` for an
affine `A`.  Since `N=mn`, the Dickson identity gives

\[
R\circ D_m=A\circ D_n\circ D_m.
\]

Substitution by the nonconstant polynomial `D_m` is injective on `C[Y]`, so
`R=A circ D_n`.  The extension is exactly

\[
D_{mn}(x,a)=D_n(D_m(x,a),a^m).
\tag{7.8}
\]

This argument classifies an arbitrary outer polynomial `R` at once.  It
therefore includes mixed aligned/nonaligned complete decompositions, repeated
prime degrees, and every sequence of Ritt moves; no induction from a
prime-degree chain theorem is required.  A degree-two Dickson factor is
affine-equivalent to a quadratic power and is absorbed by the power/Dickson
overlap before the exact-rank-two argument is invoked.

### Quadratic over a power

For `Q=(x^m+b)^2`, a block plane has a basis `U,V` satisfying

\[
U^m+V^m=-2b\ne0.
\tag{7.9}
\]

An equal block plane produces an invertible linear substitution preserving
this level.  Homogenising shows that it preserves the binary Fermat form
`U^m+V^m`.  For `m>=3`, its linear automorphism group is generated by
coordinate scalings with `m`-th power one and interchange of the coordinates.
Thus the new fibre parameter is the old one or its negative before squaring,
which is the same `Q`-fibre.  No distinct full-fusion block is created.

For `m=2`, this family is the degree-four Dickson overlap and is already
covered by (7.8).  Hence Dickson chains are the only proper rank-two-
preserving extensions.

## 8. Rank-three cubic-over-power extensions

Write

\[
Q=H(x^m),\qquad
H(Y)=Y^3+A Y^2+B Y+C,qquad m\ge2.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Exact rank three means `(A,B)!=(0,0)`, except that
`m=2`, `B=A^2/4!=0` is the rank-two Dickson overlap.  The affine-power locus
`3B=A^2` remains rank three for `m>=2`, but its cubic monodromy is `C_3` and
must be treated separately from the `S_3` branch.  In a generic fibre, the
three Kummer blocks span independent lines with generators `U_i`, where the
powered values `Y_i=U_i^m` have fixed first and second elementary symmetric
functions.

### Lemma 8.1 — Kummer-line transport on the full-kernel strata

Off the exceptional stratum `B!=0`, `A^2=4B`, `2|m`, transport between two
generic `Q`-blocks under a rank-preserving extension has the form

\[
U_i' = c_i U_{\pi(i)}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

### Proof

By Lemma 6.3, the full setwise stabiliser of every `Q`-block induces the
absolute block monodromy.  An element carrying one block to another conjugates
these full actions on the common three-dimensional span.

Off the exceptional stratum `B!=0`, `A^2=4B`, `2|m`, the Kummer rotations
form the full diagonal normal subgroup of the monomial block action.  For
`m>=3`, its three weight lines are the unique
one-dimensional imprimitivity system and hence are preserved by every such
coordinate identification.  For `m=2`, the projective block action is the
octahedral `S_4`; its projective normaliser is itself and preserves the orbit
of the three opposite-axis pairs, which are the same Kummer lines.  Thus in
the nonpower cases a block transport permutes the three weight lines, giving
(8.2).  On the affine-power locus `3B=A^2`, the projective exponent-two group
is tetrahedral `A_4`; its normaliser is generated by octahedral signed-monomial
matrices and diagonal character intertwiners, so it preserves the same axes.
The spectral argument handles this locus for `m>=3`.

\(\square\)

Put `a_i=c_i^m`.  The powered triples `Y_i` and `a_iY_i` have the same fixed
first and second symmetric functions.  On the nonpower `S_3` locus, the
constant-linear relation space of the generic cubic roots is zero when
`A!=0` and is the trace line when `A=0`.  Consequently all `a_i` are one
common scalar `c`.  The fixed coefficients give

\[
cA=A,
\qquad c^2B=B.
\tag{8.3}
\]

If `A!=0`, then `c=1`.  If `A=0` and `B!=0`, the sole nontrivial possibility
is `c=-1`.  Hence the complete fibre-equivalence group is at most `mu_2`,
and equality occurs precisely when the centred cubic is odd.  The only
proper extension is

\[
Q=x^{3m}+b x^m,
\qquad P=Q^2,
\qquad b\ne0.
\tag{8.4}
\]

On the affine-power locus, write the roots as
`Y_i=-a+omega^i w`.  Preservation of the first two elementary symmetric
functions gives

\[
\sum a_i=3,\quad \sum a_i\omega^i=0,
\quad a_1^2=a_2^2=a_3^2=1.
\]

Therefore every `a_i=1`, and this locus has no nontrivial fibre quotient.

It remains to treat the exceptional stratum excluded from Lemma 8.1.  Write
`A=2b`, `B=b^2`, and `m=2k`.  Then

\[
H(x^{2k})-C=(x^{3k}+b x^k)^2.
\]

For `k=1` this is, up to target translation, `D_6` and has rank two.  For
`k>=2` it is already the aligned-square quotient of the nonexceptional phase
`F=x^{3k}+b x^k`.  The nonexceptional calculation just completed proves that
the full rank-preserving fibre quotient of `F` has degree at most two.  Any
further nonlinear outer factor on `F^2` would therefore give a quotient of
`F` of degree greater than two, which is impossible.  No transport assertion
for the reduced Kummer kernel is needed on this stratum.

The extension (8.4) cannot repeat.  After rewriting it as a cubic over
`x^{2m}`, the outer
cubic is

\[
Y(Y+b)^2=Y^3+2bY^2+b^2Y,
\tag{8.5}
\]

which is the exceptional even stratum already isolated in the proof of Lemma
8.1.  It is already the degree-two quotient of `x^{3m}+b x^m`; a further
nonlinear quotient would compose to a rank-preserving quotient of that phase
of degree greater than two, contradicting the complete nonexceptional
fibre-equivalence calculation.

## 9. Rank-three quartic extensions

Let

\[
Q=x^4+A x^2+B x,
\qquad B\ne0.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Its monodromy is `S_4` and its root span is the standard three-dimensional
representation.

Take the rightmost indecomposable factor `G` of an outer extension `R`; then
`G circ Q` is a right factor of `R circ Q`.  Heredity forces

\[
q(Q)\le q(G\circ Q)\le q(R\circ Q)=3,
\tag{9.2}
\]

so `G circ Q` is already rank preserving.

The indecomposable-monodromy list and the length-two kernel theorems of
Behajaina--Koenig--Neftin are exhaustive:

- if `G` is an `AGL_1` polynomial, their Theorem 2.3 says that the kernel
  contains `V_4^{deg G}` in every case except the Ritt collision
  `X^3 circ ((X^3+1)X)`;
- if `G` has `S_4` monodromy, their Theorem 2.4 contains `V_4^4`;
- if `G` is nonsolvable, their Theorem 2.7 contains
  `V_4^{deg G}`.

Every displayed product contains a nonidentity element supported on one outer
block, contradicting Corollary 6.2.  Therefore only the Ritt collision can
preserve rank.  In coefficient form it occurs exactly when `A=0`:

\[
Q(cx)=cQ(x)\quad(c^3=1),
\qquad
P=Q^3=(x^4+B x)^3.
\tag{9.3}
\]

There is no further extension.  The projective monodromy of (9.3) is the
tetrahedral `S_4`; its normaliser in `PGL_3(C)` is itself because every
automorphism of `S_4` is inner.  Thus any further full-fusion transport is,
after a root permutation, scalar.  Coefficient comparison in

\[
(x^4+B x)^3=x^{12}+3Bx^9+3B^2x^6+B^3x^3
\tag{9.4}
\]

shows that a scalar semiequivariance has `c^3=1` and target multiplier one.
It is already a deck transformation and creates no distinct fibre.

## 10. Completion of the first-increase cases

The primitive audit leaves prime powers, prime Dickson factors, and quartics.
The power computation gives ranks two and three only for outer degrees two
and three.  The Dickson calculation gives the aligned half-angle identity in
rank two; its only rank-three first increase is the cubic case already covered
by the uniform Newton descent.  The quartic case is exactly the uniform
quartic descent.

By Corollary 3.2 the lower radical coefficients in those descents are
constant, so the coefficient-extraction argument applies without an analytic
qualification.  `ALL_DEGREE_STRUCTURAL_REPAIR.md` then proves:

\[
\text{cubic lift: }m=2,
\qquad
\text{quartic lift: }m=3\text{ and }A=0.
\tag{10.1}
\]

The latter is (9.3).  The former is the aligned Dickson/power-square overlap.

## 11. All-degree theorem

### Theorem 11.1 — Complex low-rank coefficient atlas

Let `P in C[x]` have degree at least two.  Up to invertible affine changes of
source and target, `q(P)<=3` if and only if `P` belongs to one of the following
families, with the lower-rank intersections removed in the stated rank:

1. rank one: powers `x^d`;
2. rank two: Dickson polynomials `D_d(x,a)` and quadratic-over-power phases
   `x^{2m}+A x^m`;
3. rank three: cubic-over-power phases
   `x^{3m}+A x^{2m}+B x^m`, indecomposable quartics
   `x^4+A x^2+B x` with `B!=0`, and the collision
   `(x^4+a x)^3` with `a!=0`.

The proper rank-preserving extensions are exactly:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{powers:}&x^m\mapsto x^{mn},\\
\text{Dickson:}&D_m(x,a)\mapsto D_{mn}(x,a),\\
\text{aligned square:}&x^{3m}+b x^m\mapsto(x^{3m}+b x^m)^2,\\
\text{quartic collision:}&x^4+a x\mapsto(x^4+a x)^3.
\end{array}
\tag{11.1}
\]

### Proof

Read ranks from the right along a complete decomposition.  Rank-one suffixes
combine to a power.  The stable-line lemma makes every nonpower rank-two or
rank-three span irreducible.  The `[2,1]` support profile would give a common
stable line, so rank cannot increase from two to three.  Proposition 5.1 and
the uniform descent classify the first strict increase above the maximal
power suffix.  Sections 7--9 classify every subsequent rank-preserving step
and show that the two rank-three steps cannot repeat.  These alternatives are
exactly the displayed families.  Direct inverse-support computations, already
sealed in the coefficient atlas and degree-32 successor, establish existence
and the stated lower-rank intersections.  \(\square\)

Combining Theorem 11.1 with (2.5) removes the last all-degree qualifier from
the algebraic fixed-seed atlas.
