## Abstract

Let \(P\in\mathbf C[x]\) have degree \(d\ge2\), and let

\[
\mathcal H_P=
\frac{\mathbf C(s)[x] \, dx}
{\{(\partial_x f+sP'f)\,dx:f\in\mathbf C(s)[x]\}}
\]

with connection

\[
\nabla_s[f\,dx]=[(\partial_s f+Pf)\,dx].
\]

Write \(q(P,dx)\) for the dimension of the cyclic subspace generated by the
fixed class \([dx]\). After affine normalisation, let

\[
P(\xi_P(t))=t^d,
\qquad
\xi_P(t)=t+O(t^{-1})
\]

be the unique inverse-at-infinity Laurent series. Split it into congruence
channels

\[
\xi_P(t)=\sum_{r\in\mathbf Z/d\mathbf Z}X_r(t),
\qquad
X_r(t)=\sum_{k\equiv r\pmod d}a_k t^k,
\]

and put \(\Sigma(P)=\{r:X_r\ne0\}\). We construct an explicit formal moment
transform at \(s=0\) and prove that it is an isomorphism of differential
modules

\[
\mathcal H_P\otimes_{\mathbf C(s)}\mathbf C((s))
\simeq
\bigoplus_{r=1}^{d-1}\mathcal K_r,
\]

where \(\mathcal K_r\) is the rank-one Kummer channel of exponent \(-r/d\).
The fixed seed has a nonzero component in \(\mathcal K_r\) exactly when
\(r\in\Sigma(P)\). A leading-term Wronskian then gives the unconditional
identity

\[
\boxed{
q(P,dx)=|\Sigma(P)|=
\dim_{\mathbf C}W_P^{\mathrm{red}},
}
\]

where \(W_P^{\mathrm{red}}\) is the reduced constant span of the inverse roots
of \(P(X)-z\). This removes stationary phase from the fixed-seed rank theorem. We also
construct fixed rapid-decay contours on every sufficiently narrow
\(s\)-sector and compute their Fourier--Gamma period-matrix asymptotic. The
resulting matrix is non-singular, so differentiation, integration by parts,
and separation of de Rham classes hold without a moving-cycle assumption or
a general duality theorem.

The same support gives a complete block-fusion law. Suppose
\(P=R\circ Q\), with \(m=\deg Q\), \(n=\deg R\), and \(d=mn\). For
\(c\in\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z\), define

\[
L_c=\{\ell\in\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z:c+m\ell\in\Sigma(P)\}.
\]

If \(V_a\) is the constant span of the roots in the \(a\)-th \(Q\)-block,
then, for every set \(A\subseteq\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\),

\[
\boxed{
\dim\sum_{a\in A}V_a
=
\sum_{c:L_c\ne\varnothing}
\operatorname{rank}
\bigl(\zeta_n^{a\ell}\bigr)_{a\in A,\,\ell\in L_c}.
}
\]

This formula determines every equality, intersection, partial fusion, and
full fusion of blocks. Equality classes of block spans form a
monodromy-invariant coarsening and therefore define a canonical actual
polynomial right factor. Along a complete decomposition, projected support
ranks form a monotone chain; at global rank at most three, no more than two
levels can create rank. Every Ritt move replaces one intermediate projection
by another and obeys an exact fibre-product constraint. At rank at most three,
exactly eleven unordered Ritt rank diamonds are possible. For

\[
P=(x^4+x)^3=H(x^3),
\qquad H(y)=y(y+1)^3,
\]

one has \(\Sigma(P)=\{1,7,10\}\subset\mathbf Z/12\mathbf Z\). Projection
modulo four gives the profile \([1,1,1]\), so the three quartic block spans
coincide. Projection modulo three gives \([3]\), so the four cubic block lines
span the same three-space. This is the complete linear explanation of the
Ritt collision.

The theorem classifies block fusion for every polynomial and every actual
polynomial decomposition. It does not, by itself, enumerate all affine
families that realise a prescribed support set; that inverse realisability
problem is separate.

# 2. Algebraic setup and affine normalisation

Let \(K=\mathbf C(s)\). For a polynomial \(P\) of degree \(d\ge2\), define

\[
D_P(f)=\partial_x f+sP'f
\]

and

\[
\mathcal H_P=K[x]dx/D_P(K[x])dx.
\]

The leading coefficient of \(sP'\) is a unit of \(K\). Repeated cancellation
of the leading monomial gives a unique representative of degree below
\(d-1\). Hence

\[
\mathcal H_P\cong K^{d-1}
\]

with basis

\[
[dx],[x\,dx],\ldots,[x^{d-2}dx].
\tag{2.1}
\]

The connection

\[
\nabla_s[f\,dx]=[(\partial_s f+Pf)\,dx]
\tag{2.2}
\]

is well defined because

\[
[\partial_s+P,\partial_x+sP']=0.
\]

For the constant seed, \(\nabla_s^j[dx]=[P^jdx]\). We define

\[
q(P,dx)=
\dim_K K\langle\nabla_s\rangle[dx].
\tag{2.3}
\]

An invertible affine source change rescales \(dx\) by a nonzero constant. A
target scaling is absorbed by a change of \(s\), while a target translation
replaces powers of \(P\) by an invertible triangular combination. Thus
\(q(P,dx)\) is invariant under invertible affine source and target changes.

We may therefore assume throughout that

\[
P(x)=x^d+\sum_{j=0}^{d-2}p_jx^j.
\tag{2.4}
\]

For convenience we also subtract \(P(0)\). This target translation does not
change any rank below. Equation (2.4) makes \(P\) monic and centred.

# 3. The inverse-at-infinity support

## Proposition 3.1 — Normalised inverse at infinity

There is a unique Laurent series

\[
\xi(t)=t+\sum_{k\le0}a_kt^k
\in t\,\mathbf C[[t^{-1}]]
\tag{3.1}
\]

such that

\[
P(\xi(t))=t^d.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Because \(P\) is centred, \(a_0=0\), so

\[
\xi(t)=t+O(t^{-1}).
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Proof

Put \(y=t^{-1}\) and \(\xi=tF(y)\), where \(F(0)=1\). Equation (3.2) becomes

\[
F^d+
\sum_{j=0}^{d-2}p_jy^{d-j}F^j=1.
\tag{3.4}
\]

At each positive power \(y^N\), the new coefficient of \(F\) enters through
\(F^d\) with coefficient \(d\), while every other term depends only on
previous coefficients. Characteristic zero gives a unique recursive solution.
The coefficient of \(t^{d-1}\) in \(P(\xi)\) is \(da_0\), hence \(a_0=0\).
\(\square\)

Let \(\zeta_d=e^{2\pi i/d}\). For
\(r\in\mathbf Z/d\mathbf Z\), put

\[
X_r(t)=
\frac1d\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\zeta_d^{-jr}\xi(\zeta_d^jt)
=
\sum_{k\equiv r\pmod d}a_kt^k.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Define the **inverse support**

\[
\Sigma(P)=\{r\in\mathbf Z/d\mathbf Z:X_r\ne0\}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

The branches of \(P(X)-z\) near infinity, with \(z=t^d\), are

\[
x_j(z)=\xi(\zeta_d^jt)
=
\sum_{r\in\mathbf Z/d\mathbf Z}\zeta_d^{jr}X_r(t).
\tag{3.7}
\]

The average of the branches is zero because \(P\) is centred. Thus

\[
X_0=0,
\qquad 0\notin\Sigma(P).
\tag{3.8}
\]

## Proposition 3.2 — Affine invariance of reduced support data

After removal of the trace channel, invertible affine source and target changes
preserve \(\Sigma(P)\) up to multiplication of its indices by a unit in
\(\mathbf Z/d\mathbf Z\). In particular, they preserve its cardinality,
every divisor-fibre partition, and all Fourier ranks used below.

### Proof

A source translation adds the same constant to every inverse branch and hence
changes only the trace channel; centring removes it. A source scaling
multiplies every non-trace channel by a nonzero constant. A target scaling
rescales \(t\). For a target translation by \(c\), the old and new
infinity parameters satisfy

\[
\widetilde t
=t(1-ct^{-d})^{1/d}
=t\,H(t^{-d}),
\qquad H(0)=1.
\]

Substitution \(t\mapsto tH(t^{-d})\) preserves each exponent residue modulo
\(d\), and its inverse has the same form, so no nonzero channel can appear or
disappear. Finally, changing the generator of the cyclic inertia group
multiplies the residue labels by a unit. \(\square\)

# 4. A direct formal moment transform

The purpose of this section is to prove the rank theorem without contour
integrals and without stationary phase.

Put \(K_0=\mathbf C((s))\). For
\(r\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}\), let

\[
\mathcal K_r=K_0e_r
\]

be the rank-one differential module with

\[
\partial_se_r=-\frac rd s^{-1}e_r.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For every \(n\in\mathbf Z\), define nonzero constants \(\mu_{r,n}\) by

\[
\mu_{r,0}=1,
\qquad
\mu_{r,n+1}=-\frac{r+nd}{d}\mu_{r,n}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

No factor in (4.2) vanishes because \(1\le r\le d-1\).

Consider a Laurent one-form, bounded above, whose exponents lie in one
congruence class:

\[
\omega=
\sum_{n\le N}b_nt^{r+nd-1}dt.
\]

Define

\[
\mathfrak M_r(\omega)=
\left(\sum_{n\le N}b_n\mu_{r,n}s^{-n}\right)e_r
\in\mathcal K_r.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is a well-defined Laurent series in \(s\): only finitely many negative
powers occur.
The pullbacks used below have coefficients with a uniform lower bound on
\(s\)-adic valuation. Thus (4.3) extends continuously and
\(\mathbf C((s))\)-linearly to those pullbacks; no uncontrolled double Laurent
series is introduced.

## Lemma 4.1 — Formal integration by parts

For every bounded-above Laurent series \(h(t)\),

\[
\mathfrak M_r\left(
\left(\partial_th+sd t^{d-1}h\right)dt
\right)=0
\tag{4.4}
\]

in each congruence channel.

### Proof

It is enough to take \(h=t^{r+nd}\). The two terms in (4.4) map to

\[
(r+nd)\mu_{r,n}s^{-n}e_r
\]

and

\[
d\mu_{r,n+1}s^{-n}e_r.
\]

Their sum is zero by (4.2). Termwise application is legitimate for a series
bounded above. \(\square\)

Pull a polynomial form back by \(x=\xi(t)\):

\[
\rho(f(x)dx)=f(\xi(t))\xi'(t)dt.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Because \(P(\xi)=t^d\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho\bigl((\partial_xq+sP'q)dx\bigr)
&=
\left(\partial_t(q(\xi))+sd t^{d-1}q(\xi)\right)dt.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.6}
\]

Lemma 4.1 therefore makes the direct sum of the moment maps descend to

\[
\mathfrak M:
\mathcal H_P\otimes_KK_0
\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_{r=1}^{d-1}\mathcal K_r.
\tag{4.7}
\]

## Lemma 4.2 — Compatibility with the \(s\)-connection

The map \(\mathfrak M\) is a morphism of differential modules.

### Proof

For a monomial channel,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_s\bigl(\mu_{r,n}s^{-n}e_r\bigr)
&=
-\left(n+\frac rd\right)
\mu_{r,n}s^{-n-1}e_r\\
&=
\mu_{r,n+1}s^{-n-1}e_r.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.8}
\]

The right-hand side is the moment of multiplication by \(t^d\). Under
\(P(\xi)=t^d\), this is exactly the action of \(P\), while coefficient
derivatives obey the ordinary product rule. Hence

\[
\mathfrak M\circ\nabla_s=\partial_s\circ\mathfrak M.
\]
\(\square\)

## Theorem 4.3 — Formal moment isomorphism

The map (4.7) is an isomorphism.

### Proof

Use the basis (2.1). From (3.3), for \(0\le j\le d-2\),

\[
\xi(t)^j\xi'(t)=t^j+O(t^{j-2}).
\tag{4.9}
\]

Write the matrix of \(\mathfrak M\) after identifying the target basis with
\(e_1,\ldots,e_{d-1}\). In row \(r\), the coefficient of \(s^0e_r\) is the
coefficient of \(t^{r-1}dt\) in (4.9). It vanishes when \(r>j+1\), and equals
one when \(r=j+1\). Terms with lower powers of \(t\) contribute positive
powers of \(s\); no negative power can occur because \(j+1<d\).

Thus the matrix lies in \(\operatorname{Mat}_{d-1}(\mathbf C[[s]])\), and its
constant term is triangular with every diagonal entry equal to one. Its
determinant is a unit. Both sides of (4.7) have rank \(d-1\) over \(K_0\), so
\(\mathfrak M\) is an isomorphism. \(\square\)

This theorem is the missing analytic bridge in algebraic form. It is stronger
for the present purpose than a stationary-phase rank comparison: it gives an
explicit channel-by-channel differential-module isomorphism and requires no
cycle, convergence, Stokes, or contour hypothesis.

# 5. The unconditional fixed-seed rank theorem

Write

\[
\xi(t)=\sum_{k\le1}a_kt^k.
\]

The pullback of the fixed seed is

\[
\xi'(t)dt=
\sum_{k\le1}ka_kt^{k-1}dt.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For \(r\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}\), its \(r\)-th moment component is

\[
v_r=
\sum_{n\in\mathbf Z}
(r+nd)a_{r+nd}\mu_{r,n}s^{-n}e_r.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The sum is bounded below as a Laurent series in \(s\). Since
\(r+nd\ne0\) and \(\mu_{r,n}\ne0\), distinct coefficients in (5.2) occupy
distinct powers of \(s\). Therefore

\[
v_r\ne0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
X_r\ne0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r\in\Sigma(P).
\tag{5.3}
\]

## Lemma 5.1 — Active Kummer channels are cyclically independent

Let \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d-1\}\), and let
\(v=(v_r)_{r\in S}\) with every \(v_r\ne0\). Then the cyclic differential
submodule generated by \(v\) in
\(\bigoplus_{r\in S}\mathcal K_r\) has rank \(|S|\).

### Proof

For each \(r\), let \(n_r\) be the largest index with nonzero coefficient in
(5.2). Then

\[
v_r=c_rs^{-n_r}e_r(1+O(s)),
\qquad c_r\ne0.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Put

\[
\alpha_r=-n_r-\frac rd
=-\frac{r+n_rd}{d}.
\]

The numbers \(\alpha_r\) are distinct because their numerators are distinct
modulo \(d\). For \(j\ge0\),

\[
\partial_s^jv_r
=
c_r(\alpha_r)_{\underline j}
 s^{-n_r-j}e_r(1+O(s)),
\tag{5.5}
\]

where \((u)_{\underline j}=u(u-1)\cdots(u-j+1)\). The determinant of the first
\(|S|\) derivative columns has leading coefficient

\[
\left(\prod_{r\in S}c_r\right)
\prod_{r<r'}(\alpha_{r'}-\alpha_r),
\tag{5.6}
\]

up to a nonzero monomial in \(s\). This is nonzero. Thus the first \(|S|\)
derivatives are independent. The ambient active direct sum has rank \(|S|\),
so the cyclic rank is exactly \(|S|\). \(\square\)

## Theorem 5.2 — Unconditional support formula

For every polynomial \(P\in\mathbf C[x]\) of degree at least two,

\[
\boxed{q(P,dx)=|\Sigma(P)|.}
\tag{5.7}
\]

### Proof

Affine-normalise \(P\). By Theorem 4.3, extending scalars from \(K\) to
\(K_0\) identifies the cyclic submodule generated by \([dx]\) with the cyclic
submodule generated by the vector \((v_r)\). Scalar extension preserves rank.
Equation (5.3) identifies the active summands with \(\Sigma(P)\), and Lemma
5.1 gives their rank. \(\square\)

# 6. Equality with the reduced inverse-root span

Let \(W_P^{\mathrm{red}}\) be the constant \(\mathbf C\)-span of the inverse
roots of \(P(X)-z\), with the trace line removed. In centred coordinates the
trace is already zero, so we write \(W_P\).

## Theorem 6.1 — Root span as inverse support

\[
\boxed{\dim_{\mathbf C}W_P=|\Sigma(P)|.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Proof

Equation (3.7) expresses the vector of roots
\((x_0,\ldots,x_{d-1})\) as the discrete Fourier transform of
\((X_0,\ldots,X_{d-1})\). The Fourier matrix is invertible. The nonzero
\(X_r\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbf C\), because their Laurent
supports occupy disjoint residue classes modulo \(d\). Hence the root span has
dimension equal to the number of nonzero channels. Equation (3.8) removes the
trace channel. \(\square\)

Combining Theorems 5.2 and 6.1 gives the main unconditional identity

\[
\boxed{
q(P,dx)=|\Sigma(P)|=
\dim_{\mathbf C}W_P^{\mathrm{red}}.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

The conductor equality in the child release remains a valid independent
representation-theoretic explanation. It is no longer needed to establish
(6.2).

## Corollary 6.2 — Inverse support is the infinity-character spectrum

Let \(\tau\) be the full cycle at infinity, indexed by
\(\tau(x_j)=x_{j+1}\). Then

\[
\tau(X_r)=\zeta_d^r X_r.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Hence \(\Sigma(P)\) is exactly the set of nontrivial inertia characters that
occur in \(W_P^{\mathrm{red}}\), each with multiplicity one. Replacing the
generator of inertia multiplies the indices by a unit modulo \(d\). The
projection laws for polynomial right factors are therefore character
restriction laws for subgroups of the cyclic inertia group.

### Proof

Apply \(\tau\) to the Fourier inverse
\(X_r=d^{-1}\sum_j\zeta_d^{-jr}x_j\) and reindex. Distinct channels are
one-dimensional by their disjoint Laurent supports. \(\square\)

# 7. Explicit analytic realisation without a moving-cycle gap

The algebraic rank theorem is complete without period integrals. This section
constructs the required rapid-decay cycles explicitly and proves that their
period matrix is non-singular.

Assume that

\[
P(x)=x^d+O(x^{d-2})
\]

is monic and centred. Fix \(\theta_0\in\mathbf R\) and
\(0<\varepsilon<\pi/2\), and let

\[
\mathfrak S=\{s\in\mathbf C^*:|\arg s-\theta_0|<\varepsilon\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

For \(k\in\mathbf Z/d\mathbf Z\), put

\[
\phi_k=\frac{\pi-\theta_0+2\pi k}{d}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Let \(\rho_k\) be the ray \(x=re^{i\phi_k}\), \(r\ge0\), and let
\(\Gamma_k\) run from infinity to the origin along \(\rho_k\), then from the
origin to infinity along \(\rho_{k+1}\).

## Proposition 7.1 — Uniform rapid decay

For every compact subsector \(K\Subset\mathfrak S\), there are constants
\(R,c,C>0\) such that

\[
\Re(sP(re^{i\phi_k}))\le -cr^d+C
\tag{7.3}
\]

for every \(s\in K\), every \(r\ge R\), and every \(k\).

### Proof

By construction,

\[
\Re(se^{id\phi_k})
=-|s|\cos(\arg s-\theta_0).
\]

On a compact subsector this is bounded above by a negative constant. The
centred lower terms are \(O(r^{d-2})\), uniformly in the finitely many chosen
directions, and are absorbed by half of the leading negative term for large
\(r\). \(\square\)

For every polynomial \(A\), the integral

\[
I_{k,A}(s)=\int_{\Gamma_k}A(x)e^{sP(x)}\,dx
\tag{7.4}
\]

therefore converges absolutely and locally uniformly. The chains are fixed
throughout \(\mathfrak S\), so differentiation produces no chain-velocity
term.

## Proposition 7.2 — Differentiation and twisted exactness

For every \(j\ge0\),

\[
\partial_s^jI_{k,A}(s)
=\int_{\Gamma_k}P(x)^jA(x)e^{sP(x)}\,dx.
\tag{7.5}
\]

For every polynomial \(f\),

\[
\int_{\Gamma_k}(\partial_xf+sP'f)e^{sP(x)}\,dx=0.
\tag{7.6}
\]

### Proof

On compact subsectors, Proposition 7.1 dominates every differentiated
integrand by \(C(1+r)^Ne^{-cr^d}\). Dominated differentiation proves (7.5).
For (7.6), the integrand is \(d(fe^{sP})\). The two finite endpoints at the
origin cancel, and rapid decay kills both ends at infinity. \(\square\)

Radially compactify the affine line. The \(d\) chosen decay directions are
marked boundary points. The resulting relative-chain model gives

\[
H_1^{\mathrm{rd}}(\mathbf A^1,e^{sP})
\simeq
\widetilde H_0(\{\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_{d-1}\};\mathbf C),
\tag{7.7}
\]

with \([\Gamma_k]\mapsto e_{k+1}-e_k\). Hence
\([\Gamma_0],\ldots,[\Gamma_{d-2}]\) form a basis and
\(\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}[\Gamma_k]=0\).

For \(0\le k,j\le d-2\), define

\[
\Pi_{k,j}(s)=\int_{\Gamma_k}x^je^{sP(x)}\,dx.
\tag{7.8}
\]

## Theorem 7.3 — Fourier--Gamma period determinant

Let \(\zeta_d=e^{2\pi i/d}\) and put \(r=j+1\). As
\(s=\rho e^{i\theta_0}\to0\) on the central ray,

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{\rho\downarrow0}s^{r/d}\Pi_{k,j}(s)
=\frac{\Gamma(r/d)}d e^{\pi ir/d}
 (\zeta_d^r-1)\zeta_d^{kr}.
}
\tag{7.9}
\]

Consequently

\[
\det\Pi(s)=C_d s^{-(d-1)/2}(1+o(1)),
\qquad C_d\ne0.
\tag{7.10}
\]

### Proof

Choose the branch of \(s^{1/d}\) on \(\mathfrak S\) and put
\(u=s^{1/d}x\). Then

\[
sP(x)=u^d+\sum_{j=0}^{d-2}p_js^{1-j/d}u^j.
\]

On each scaled ray, the leading term has real part \(-|u|^d\). Young's
inequality absorbs every lower term into a fixed fraction of \(|u|^d\), so
dominated convergence applies. The scaled ray angles are
\(\psi_k=(\pi+2\pi k)/d\). The integral from the origin to infinity on a ray
of angle \(\psi\) is

\[
\frac1d\Gamma(r/d)e^{ir\psi}.
\]

Taking the oriented difference of the two rays gives (7.9). Its constant
matrix is a non-singular diagonal matrix multiplied by

\[
(\zeta_d^{kr})_{0\le k\le d-2,\ 1\le r\le d-1},
\]

which is a Vandermonde matrix on the distinct numbers
\(\zeta_d,\ldots,\zeta_d^{d-1}\). Finally,
\(\sum_{r=1}^{d-1}r/d=(d-1)/2\), proving (7.10). \(\square\)

## Corollary 7.4 — Unconditional period interpretation

The period matrix \(\Pi(s)\) is invertible throughout \(\mathfrak S\). Every
relation in \(\mathcal H_P\) therefore gives the corresponding differential
relation for the complete rapid-decay period system, and no additional
relation vanishes against all of these cycles.

### Proof

Theorem 7.3 gives invertibility at sufficiently small points of the central
ray. Proposition 7.2 makes \(\Pi\) a fundamental solution matrix for the
first-order connection on \(\mathcal H_P\). Its determinant satisfies a
scalar first-order equation on \(\mathfrak S\), so a nonzero value cannot
develop a zero. \(\square\)

This directly closes convergence, differentiation, integration by parts, and
separation of classes. General rapid-decay duality and stationary phase are
consistent external frameworks, but neither is load-bearing here.

## Corollary 7.5 — Universal monodromy at \(s=0\)

Analytic continuation once anticlockwise around \(s=0\) sends

\[
[\Gamma_k]\longmapsto[\Gamma_{k-1}]
\tag{7.11}
\]

with indices modulo \(d\). In the basis
\([\Gamma_0],\ldots,[\Gamma_{d-2}]\), one has

\[
[\Gamma_{d-1}]=-
\sum_{j=0}^{d-2}[\Gamma_j].
\]

The monodromy characteristic polynomial is therefore

\[
\frac{\lambda^d-1}{\lambda-1},
\tag{7.12}
\]

and its eigenvalues are \(\zeta_d^{-r}\), \(1\le r\le d-1\). These are
exactly the monodromies of the Kummer channels in Theorem 4.3. This computes
the sectorial continuation relevant at \(s=0\). Stokes matrices at
\(s=\infty\) contain additional critical-value data, but are not hypotheses
for the rank or period-relation theorems.

A chosen single cycle can still annihilate a nonzero differential submodule
of the fixed-seed cyclic module. Its scalar period has minimal order
\(q(P,dx)\) over \(\mathbf C(s)\) exactly when the induced differential-module
map on that cyclic module has zero kernel, equivalently when no nonzero class
in the module has identically zero period on the chosen cycle. This is a
non-annihilation condition on that cycle, not an analytic hypothesis.

# 8. Block spans for a polynomial decomposition

Suppose

\[
P=R\circ Q,
\qquad
m=\deg Q,
\qquad
n=\deg R,
\qquad
d=mn.
\tag{8.1}
\]

After affine changes between the factors, assume that \(Q\), \(R\), and
\(P\) are monic and that \(Q\) is centred. Then \(P\) is centred as well.

Index the roots of \(P(X)-t^d\) by

\[
x_j=\xi(\zeta_d^jt),
\qquad j\in\mathbf Z/d\mathbf Z.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Since

\[
Q(x_j)\sim x_j^m\sim\zeta_n^jt^m,
\]

the \(Q\)-blocks are

\[
B_a=\{a+nb:b\in\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z\},
\qquad a\in\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Let

\[
V_a=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbf C}\{x_j:j\in B_a\}
\subseteq W_P.
\tag{8.4}
\]

For \(c\in\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z\), define

\[
L_c=
\{\ell\in\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z:c+m\ell\in\Sigma(P)\}
\tag{8.5}
\]

and

\[
E_c=
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbf C}
\{X_{c+m\ell}:\ell\in L_c\}.
\tag{8.6}
\]

The spaces \(E_c\) form a direct sum because they have disjoint Laurent
supports modulo \(m\).

## Theorem 8.1 — Fourier block decomposition

For every \(a\in\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\),

\[
V_a=
\bigoplus_{c:L_c\ne\varnothing}
\mathbf C\,Y_{a,c},
\tag{8.7}
\]

where

\[
Y_{a,c}=
\sum_{\ell\in L_c}\zeta_n^{a\ell}X_{c+m\ell}
\in E_c.
\tag{8.8}
\]

A harmless nonzero factor \(\zeta_d^{ac}\) has been omitted from (8.8).

### Proof

Using (3.7), for \(b\in\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
x_{a+nb}
&=
\sum_{r\in\Sigma(P)}
\zeta_d^{(a+nb)r}X_r\\
&=
\sum_c
\zeta_m^{bc}\zeta_d^{ac}
\sum_{\ell\in L_c}
\zeta_n^{a\ell}X_{c+m\ell}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.9}
\]

The discrete Fourier transform in \(b\) is invertible and isolates one value
of \(c\) at a time. Thus the span of the block roots is the span of the
vectors in (8.8). Vectors for different \(c\) lie in the direct summands
\(E_c\). Each \(Y_{a,c}\) is nonzero because the \(X_{c+m\ell}\) are
independent. This proves (8.7). \(\square\)

## Theorem 8.2 — Complete block-fusion rank formula

For every subset \(A\subseteq\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\),

\[
\boxed{
\dim\sum_{a\in A}V_a
=
\sum_{c:L_c\ne\varnothing}
\operatorname{rank}
\bigl(\zeta_n^{a\ell}\bigr)_{a\in A,\,\ell\in L_c}.
}
\tag{8.10}
\]

### Proof

Inside \(E_c\), the vectors \(Y_{a,c}\) have coefficient rows
\((\zeta_n^{a\ell})_{\ell\in L_c}\) in the independent basis
\((X_{c+m\ell})_{\ell\in L_c}\). Their span therefore has the displayed
matrix rank. The summands \(E_c\) are direct, so their dimensions add.
\(\square\)

Equation (8.10) is the promised complete classification. It determines every
partial sum, intersection, coincidence, and rank increase among the blocks.
The following explicit consequences are often easier to use.

## Corollary 8.3 — Exact right-factor heredity

Every block has dimension

\[
\dim V_a=
\#\{c:L_c\ne\varnothing\}.
\tag{8.11}
\]

Since one block is the root span of \(Q(X)-y\) after a transcendental base
change,

\[
\boxed{
q(Q,dx)=\dim W_Q
=|\pi_m(\Sigma(P))|,
}
\tag{8.12}
\]

where \(\pi_m:\mathbf Z/d\mathbf Z\to\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z\) is reduction
modulo \(m\). In particular, \(q(Q,dx)\le q(P,dx)\).

## Corollary 8.4 — Pairwise intersections

For \(a,b\in\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\),

\[
\boxed{
\dim(V_a\cap V_b)
=
\#\left\{
 c:L_c\ne\varnothing,
\ (a-b)(\ell-\ell')\equiv0\pmod n
\ \forall\ell,\ell'\in L_c
\right\}.
}
\tag{8.13}
\]

Indeed, in each \(E_c\), the two one-dimensional lines coincide exactly when
their coefficient vectors are proportional.

## Corollary 8.5 — Equality stabiliser

Define

\[
H_m(P)=
\left\{
\delta\in\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z:
\delta(\ell-\ell')\equiv0\pmod n
\quad
\forall c,\ \ell,\ell'\in L_c
\right\}.
\tag{8.14}
\]

Then \(H_m(P)\) is a subgroup and

\[
V_a=V_b
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
b-a\in H_m(P).
\tag{8.15}
\]

The number of distinct block spans is

\[
[\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z:H_m(P)].
\tag{8.16}
\]

## Corollary 8.6 — Full fusion

The following are equivalent:

1. all block spans coincide;
2. every block span equals \(W_P\);
3. \(q(P,dx)=q(Q,dx)\);
4. every nonempty \(L_c\) has size one;
5. the projection \(\pi_m:\Sigma(P)\to\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z\) is injective.

Thus full fusion has an exact character-theoretic criterion.

## Corollary 8.7 — No fusion

The block spaces \(V_0,\ldots,V_{n-1}\) form a direct sum if and only if every
nonempty \(L_c\) equals all of \(\mathbf Z/n\mathbf Z\). Equivalently,

\[
q(P,dx)=nq(Q,dx).
\tag{8.17}
\]

Every intermediate fusion pattern is measured by the Fourier ranks in
(8.10).

# 9. All complete decompositions and Ritt moves

Let

\[
P=P_1\circ P_2\circ\cdots\circ P_k
\tag{9.1}
\]

be a complete decomposition into indecomposable polynomials. For each suffix

\[
Q_i=P_i\circ\cdots\circ P_k,
\qquad m_i=\deg Q_i,
\tag{9.2}
\]

the corresponding block system and every fusion datum are obtained by
projecting the one fixed support set

\[
\Sigma(P)\subseteq\mathbf Z/d\mathbf Z
\]

modulo \(m_i\) and applying Theorem 8.2.

This already handles every partial decomposition: any right factor can be
refined to a complete suffix. A full cycle of length \(d\) has a unique block
system of each specified block size, so two right factors of the same degree
induce the same partition at infinity, up to relabelling.

Ritt's first theorem states that any two complete decompositions of a complex
polynomial are connected by adjacent Ritt moves and inserted affine changes.
Affine changes preserve the reduced support data. In a nontrivial adjacent
move, two coprime indecomposable degrees \(a\) and \(b\) are interchanged. If
the fixed suffix to their right has degree \(h\), the move changes the one
intermediate suffix degree

\[
bh\longleftrightarrow ah,
\tag{9.3}
\]

while keeping \(h\) and \(abh\) fixed.

## Theorem 9.1 — Ritt-invariant fusion classification

The complete block-fusion data of \(P\), across every complete decomposition
and every sequence of Ritt moves, are the collection of Fourier rank systems

\[
\mathcal F_m(P)=
\left\{
\operatorname{rank}
(\zeta_{d/m}^{a\ell})_{a\in A,\ell\in L_c}
:
A\subseteq\mathbf Z/(d/m)\mathbf Z,
\ c\in\mathbf Z/m\mathbf Z
\right\}
\tag{9.4}
\]

for the suffix degrees \(m\) occurring in the Ritt decomposition graph.
Every \(\mathcal F_m(P)\) is computed from the same invariant
\(\Sigma(P)\). Under an adjacent move (9.3), the fusion system
\(\mathcal F_{bh}(P)\) is replaced by \(\mathcal F_{ah}(P)\); all other levels
remain unchanged.

### Proof

Theorem 8.2 gives \(\mathcal F_m(P)\) for each actual suffix degree. Ritt's
first theorem connects all complete decompositions by moves that change only
one intermediate suffix degree. Since the total polynomial and its inverse
support do not change, the stated projection replacement is exact. Conversely,
every block equality or rank in any decomposition is one of the ranks in
(9.4). \(\square\)

When \(\gcd(a,b)=1\), the Chinese remainder theorem views the characters in a
moved \(ab\)-block on a two-coordinate grid. The monomial and Chebyshev forms
in Ritt's second theorem determine when the polynomial move exists; once it
exists, Theorem 9.1 determines the accompanying fusion by reading the same
characters along the other coordinate projection. The linear classification
therefore requires no separate monomial, Chebyshev, or exceptional fusion
case.


## 9.2 Canonical fusion quotient

Let \(\mathcal B\) be the block system induced by a decomposition
\(P=R\circ Q\). For a block \(B\), put

\[
V(B)=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbf C}\{x_i:i\in B\},
\]

and declare \(B\sim B'\) when \(V(B)=V(B')\).

### Theorem 9.2 — Equality fusion exposes an actual factor

The unions of the \(\sim\)-classes form a monodromy-invariant block system.
Consequently there is a polynomial factorisation

\[
P=R^{\sharp}\circ Q^{\sharp},
\qquad Q^{\sharp}=S\circ Q,
\tag{9.5}
\]

unique up to an affine change of intermediate variable, whose blocks are
exactly those unions. Distinct \(Q^{\sharp}\)-blocks have distinct root spans.
If \(m=\deg Q\), \(n=\deg R\), and \(H_m(P)\) is (8.14), then

\[
\boxed{
\deg Q^{\sharp}=m|H_m(P)|,
\qquad
\deg R^{\sharp}=n/|H_m(P)|.
}
\tag{9.6}
\]

### Proof

For geometric monodromy \(G\), one has \(gV(B)=V(gB)\). Equality of spans is
therefore \(G\)-invariant, and its equivalence-class unions form a block
system. The intermediate-field correspondence and Lüroth's theorem give a
rational right factor; total ramification over infinity makes it polynomial.
Because the new partition coarsens the \(Q\)-partition, the factor is
\(S\circ Q\). In the cyclic infinity indexing, the equality classes are the
cosets of \(H_m(P)\), giving (9.6). \(\square\)

Repeated equality of block spans is thus never merely a numerical accident.
It identifies a canonical hidden factor, although full fusion can produce only
the trivial factor \(Q^{\sharp}=P\).

For a partial-fusion control, view

\[
P(x)=x^8+x^4
\]

through the right factor \(Q=x^2\). Its support is \(\{1,5\}\) modulo eight.
The four \(Q\)-block lines occur in two equal pairs, with
\(H_2(P)=\{0,2\}\subset\mathbf Z/4\mathbf Z\). The theorem recovers the
coarser actual right factor \(Q^{\sharp}=x^4\), of degree
\(2|H_2(P)|=4\), and an outer quadratic.

## 9.3 Fusion defects along a decomposition chain

For a complete decomposition (9.1), let \(Q_i\) and \(m_i\) be as in (9.2),
and put

\[
r_i=q(Q_i,dx)=|\pi_{m_i}\Sigma(P)|.
\tag{9.7}
\]

Then \(r_1\ge r_2\ge\cdots\ge r_k\). Define
\(\delta_i=r_i-r_{i+1}\). Projection gives

\[
\boxed{
q(P,dx)-q(P_k,dx)=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\delta_i.
}
\tag{9.8}
\]

Moreover, \(\delta_i=0\) exactly when the level
\(Q_i=P_i\circ Q_{i+1}\) has full fusion.

### Corollary 9.3 — Low-rank chain sparsity

If \(q(P,dx)\le3\), every complete decomposition has at most two
rank-increasing levels above a rightmost power, at most one above a rightmost
Chebyshev factor, and none above a rightmost rank-three quartic. Every other
level is a rank-preserving full-fusion extension.

## 9.4 Ritt diamonds

Consider an adjacent nontrivial Ritt move that interchanges coprime degrees
\(a,b\) above a fixed suffix of degree \(h\). Put

\[
r_0=|\pi_h\Sigma(P)|,
\quad r_a=|\pi_{ah}\Sigma(P)|,
\quad r_b=|\pi_{bh}\Sigma(P)|,
\quad r_2=|\pi_{abh}\Sigma(P)|.
\tag{9.9}
\]

### Theorem 9.4 — Ritt fibre-product constraint

Let \(S=\pi_{abh}\Sigma(P)\). For each occupied residue \(c\) modulo \(h\),
put

\[
S_c=\{u\in S:u\equiv c\pmod h\},
\quad A_c=\pi_{ah}(S_c),
\quad B_c=\pi_{bh}(S_c).
\tag{9.10}
\]

The map \(u\mapsto(u\bmod ah,u\bmod bh)\) is injective on each \(S_c\).
Hence

\[
\boxed{
|S_c|\le |A_c|\,|B_c|
\quad\text{and}\quad
r_2\le\sum_c|A_c|\,|B_c|.
}
\tag{9.11}
\]

### Proof

Equal images modulo \(ah\) and \(bh\) imply equality modulo
\(\operatorname{lcm}(ah,bh)=abh\), since \(\gcd(a,b)=1\). \(\square\)

No universal additive inequality follows: for
\((a,b,h)=(2,3,1)\) and \(S=\{0,1,3,4\}\), one has
\((r_0,r_a,r_b,r_2)=(1,2,2,4)\).

### Theorem 9.5 — Complete low-rank Ritt table

If \(r_2\le3\), the unordered pair \(\{r_a,r_b\}\) is exactly one of the
following eleven possibilities.

| Top \(r_2\) | Bottom \(r_0\) | Intermediate ranks \(\{r_a,r_b\}\) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | \(\{1,1\}\) |
| 2 | 1 | \(\{1,2\}\) |
| 2 | 1 | \(\{2,2\}\) |
| 2 | 2 | \(\{2,2\}\) |
| 3 | 1 | \(\{1,3\}\) |
| 3 | 1 | \(\{2,2\}\) |
| 3 | 1 | \(\{2,3\}\) |
| 3 | 1 | \(\{3,3\}\) |
| 3 | 2 | \(\{2,3\}\) |
| 3 | 2 | \(\{3,3\}\) |
| 3 | 3 | \(\{3,3\}\) |

### Proof

The maps to residues modulo \(ah\) and \(bh\) jointly separate the elements
of \(S\). For one element the result is immediate. For two elements, two
rank-one intermediate partitions cannot jointly separate them. For three
elements, a rank-one intermediate forces the other to have rank three; over a
two-block bottom partition, two rank-two refinements coincide and therefore
cannot have discrete common refinement; and over a one-block bottom, two
distinct rank-two partitions can have discrete common refinement. These cases
are exactly the displayed rows. \(\square\)

The table exhausts the rank behaviour of actual low-rank Ritt moves. It does
not assert that each row is realised by a polynomial identity. For the
collision in Section 11, \((a,b,h)=(3,4,1)\) and

\[
(r_0;r_a\leftrightarrow r_b;r_2)
=(1;3\leftrightarrow1;3).
\tag{9.12}
\]

# 10. Complete low-rank fusion profiles

Suppose \(q(P,dx)=|\Sigma(P)|\le3\). For a fixed right-factor degree \(m\),
the nonzero fibre sizes \((|L_c|)\) form a partition of \(q(P,dx)\). Hence the
following list is exhaustive.

| Global rank | Fibre-size profile | Right-factor rank \(q(Q,dx)\) | Block geometry |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 1 | \([1]\) | 1 | Every block line is the same line. |
| 2 | \([1,1]\) | 2 | Every block plane equals \(W_P\). |
| 2 | \([2]\) | 1 | Block lines form a Fourier orbit spanning a plane. |
| 3 | \([1,1,1]\) | 3 | Every block three-space equals \(W_P\). |
| 3 | \([2,1]\) | 2 | Block planes share the singleton channel; the other line moves in a two-channel plane. |
| 3 | \([3]\) | 1 | Block lines form a Fourier orbit spanning a three-space. |

The equality stabiliser (8.14) tells how many geometrically distinct blocks
occur inside each profile. Thus the table is a complete structural
classification, not merely a list of possible dimensions.

This table combines with the child release's indecomposable theorem as follows.
Every indecomposable suffix of a rank-at-most-three phase is a power, a
Chebyshev polynomial, or an indecomposable quartic. The table then describes
all ways in which that suffix can sit inside every larger decomposition. What
remains outside the present theorem is an affine **realisability atlas**:
which coefficient families realise each admissible support and decomposition
lattice.

# 11. The generating Ritt collision

Put

\[
Q(x)=x^4+x,
\qquad
P(x)=Q(x)^3.
\tag{11.1}
\]

There are two decompositions:

\[
P=y^3\circ Q
\tag{11.2}
\]

and

\[
P=H\circ x^3,
\qquad
H(y)=y(y+1)^3.
\tag{11.3}
\]

The inverse at infinity satisfies

\[
\xi(t)^4+\xi(t)=t^4,
\tag{11.4}
\]

with expansion

\[
\xi(t)=
 t-\frac14t^{-2}-\frac1{32}t^{-5}+\cdots.
\tag{11.5}
\]

Let \(\omega^3=1\). Equation (11.4) gives

\[
\xi(\omega t)=\omega\xi(t),
\tag{11.6}
\]

so every support exponent is congruent to one modulo three. The quartic trace
is zero, so no support exponent is congruent to zero modulo four. The first
three nonzero terms in (11.5) occupy residues

\[
1,10,7\pmod{12}.
\]

The two congruence restrictions leave no fourth permitted residue. Hence

\[
\boxed{\Sigma(P)=\{1,7,10\}.}
\tag{11.7}
\]

## 11.1 Quartic-block view

For the right factor \(Q\), \(m=4\) and \(n=3\). Reduction modulo four sends

\[
1\mapsto1,
\qquad
7\mapsto3,
\qquad
10\mapsto2.
\]

Thus the fibre profile is

\[
[1,1,1].
\]

By Corollary 8.6, the three quartic block spans are all equal to \(W_P\), and

\[
q(P,dx)=q(Q,dx)=3.
\tag{11.8}
\]

## 11.2 Cubic-block view after the Ritt move

For the right factor \(x^3\), \(m=3\) and \(n=4\). All three support residues
map to one modulo three. Thus the profile is

\[
[3].
\]

Every cubic block has rank one, the four block lines are distinct, and their
sum has rank three. Formula (8.13) gives zero intersection for distinct lines.

The Ritt move therefore changes

\[
[1,1,1]\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad[3]
\tag{11.9}
\]

by replacing projection modulo four with projection modulo three. The global
rank and support remain fixed. This is the precise mechanism that the naive
inheritance bound missed.

# 12. Exact finite computation

The accompanying program `src/fusion_support.py` implements two independent
sides of the main identity for trusted rational input.

1. It computes \(q(P,dx)\) by exact twisted-Krylov reduction over
   \(\mathbf Q(s)\), returning a nonzero independence minor and the first
   dependence relation when one occurs.
2. It recursively solves (3.4) for the inverse-at-infinity coefficients. It
   stops when it has witnessed \(q(P,dx)\) distinct non-trace residue classes.
   Theorem 5.2 proves that no further class can occur.
3. For every divisor \(m\mid d\) with \(m\ge2\), it computes the support fibres \(L_c\), the
   right-factor rank predicted when a degree-\(m\) right factor exists, the
   equality stabiliser, pairwise intersection dimensions, and the full-fusion
   and direct-sum criteria.

The program does not assert that every divisor is an actual right-factor
degree and does not enumerate the decomposition lattice. Standard polynomial
decomposition algorithms can supply that lattice; the support formula then
classifies every node and edge.

The frozen tests include:

| Phase | Degree | Exact \(q\) | \(\Sigma(P)\) |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| \(x^4+x\) | 4 | 3 | \(\{1,2,3\}\) |
| \((x^4+x)^3\) | 12 | 3 | \(\{1,7,10\}\) |
| \((x^3+x)^2\) | 6 | 2 | \(\{1,5\}\) |
| \(T_5=16x^5-20x^3+5x\) | 5 | 2 | \(\{1,4\}\) |
| \(x^6+x^2+1\) | 6 | 3 | \(\{1,3,5\}\) |
| \((x^3+x)^2+(x^3+x)\) | 6 | 4 | \(\{1,2,4,5\}\) |

The degree-twelve receipt checks both Ritt views in Sections 11.1 and 11.2.
The negative control raises the rank from two to four and changes the support
fibres from \([1,1]\) to \([2,2]\).

A separate clean-room control in `cleanroom/fraction_audit.py` imports neither
the production module nor a computer-algebra library. Exact `Fraction`
arithmetic checks nine named phases, twelve deterministic random phases, four
composition projections, the Ritt fibre-product constraint, and all eleven
low-rank diamond types. It specialises the Krylov calculation at four
nonzero rational values of \(s\), so it is an independent fault control rather
than a symbolic proof over \(\mathbf Q(s)\).

# 13. Assurance, novelty, and remaining work

## 13.1 Closed in this candidate

Subject to ordinary mathematical checking of the displayed proofs, this note
closes the following implications:

- the fixed-seed rank/root-span equality has no analytic or stationary-phase
  hypothesis;
- fixed sectorial rapid-decay contours give an unconditional analytic
  realisation, with an explicit non-singular Fourier--Gamma period matrix;
- block fusion for every actual decomposition is exactly the Fourier rank law
  (8.10);
- every block equality and intersection has the explicit criteria
  (8.13)–(8.16);
- all complete decompositions and Ritt moves are classified by congruence
  projections of one invariant support set;
- equality fusion exposes a canonical actual polynomial factor;
- rank-at-most-three chains have at most two rank-creation levels and every
  adjacent move lies in the eleven-row Ritt table; and
- the two faces of \((x^4+x)^3\) are the profiles \([1,1,1]\) and \([3]\).

## 13.2 Not closed by this candidate

The following are distinct questions and are not claimed here:

- an affine coefficient-family enumeration of every polynomial with
  \(|\Sigma(P)|\le3\);
- a classification of which abstract support subsets are polynomially
  realisable;
- a new proof of Ritt's decomposition theorems;
- external specialist verification, formal verification, or independent
  reproduction;
- settled novelty, priority, or field-wide significance; and
- a guarantee that an arbitrarily chosen non-rapid-decay contour defines a
  period, or that every single rapid-decay contour is dual-cyclic.

The first two items form an inverse realisability problem. They are no longer
needed to classify fusion once \(P\) and a decomposition are given, but they
would be needed for a closed-form affine atlas of all low-rank phases.
