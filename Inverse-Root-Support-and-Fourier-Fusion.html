14. Consequences and research directions

14.1 Generic minimal ODE order: proved corollary

For a rapid-decay cycle Γ\Gamma, put

IΓ(s)=∫ΓesP(x)dx. I_\Gamma(s)=\int_\Gamma e^{sP(x)}\,dx.

Every differential relation in the cyclic module annihilates every such period. For a generic functional on the complete period solution space, Theorems 5.2 and 7.3 give minimal scalar differential-equation order

q(P,dx)=|Σ(P)|.(14.1) q(P,dx)=|\Sigma(P)|. \qquad\text{(14.1)}

The word “generic” is essential: a particular cycle can annihilate an active channel. The supplied exploratory reducer produces order-three operators for (x6+x2)2(x^6+x^2)^2 and (x4+x)3(x^4+x)^3, while a generic degree-twelve phase has order eleven. Those demonstrations illustrate (14.1), but their numerical quadrature and fail-open script are not additional theorem evidence.

14.2 Zero-cycle Fourier channels: formulation, not closure

For weights njn_j with zero sum, an identity

∑jnjg(xj(t))=0(14.2) \sum_jn_jg(x_j(t))=0 \qquad\text{(14.2)}

is equivalent to vanishing of the corresponding Fourier channels of g∘ξg\circ\xi. This formulation suggests exact tests for selected zero-cycle identities. In the degree-twelve collision, the supplied script identifies the candidate centre elements

g=x7+74x4,g=x10−52x4. g=x^7+\frac74x^4, \qquad g=x^{10}-\frac52x^4.

No theorem in this package proves a complete zero-cycle criterion or resolves a named Gavrilov–Pakovich problem. The script prints REVIEW on a borderline comparison and returns success, so it is excluded from the release gate.

14.3 Laurent-polynomial extension: separate programme

For a chosen local branch of a Laurent polynomial, inverse-series channels turn residue moments into coefficient exclusions. The supplied small-bidegree experiments may be useful, but their control dimensions do not presently agree and the harness does not fail on the discrepancy. These materials are preserved under source history and are not evidence for a Laurent classification theorem.

15. Theorem-level relation to prior work

Sabbah [Sa99] places twisted de Rham complexes in a broad algebraic setting. Bloch and Esnault [BE04] and Hien [Hi09] establish rapid-decay/de Rham pairings for irregular connections; Hien and Roucairol [HR08] give integral representations for exponential Gauss–Manin systems. These works explain the analytic environment of Sections 2 and 7. The present claim is narrower and more explicit: for one-variable polynomial phases and the fixed class [dx][dx], Theorem 4.3 identifies the completed module channel by channel, and Theorem 5.2 reads its cyclic rank directly from inverse support. The targeted search found no statement of that fixed-seed formula in those sources.

Ritt [Ri22] supplies the decomposition moves used in Section 9, while Müller and Zieve [MZ08] give a modern structural treatment. Burda and Khovanskii [BK12] use primitive polynomial monodromy in a bounded-radical classification. The recent work of Behajaina, König and Neftin [BKN26] classifies a largeness alternative and explicit exceptions for monodromy groups of compositions of two indecomposable polynomials; it is directly relevant to a future all-degree proof of Conjecture 13.2. None of these sources, on the materials examined, states the all-block Fourier rank formula (8.10).

Girstmair [Gi99, Gi03] is a direct antecedent for constant relations among polynomial roots; the 2003 corrigendum is included. The Fourier support calculus concerns the reduced span of all local inverse roots and equality of entire decomposition blocks. It does not classify every possible root relation.

Álvarez Sánchez, Bravo Trinidad and Mardešić [ABM13] show, in the zero-dimensional Abelian-integral setting, that vanishing phenomena need not be sums of composition terms. Pakovich and Muzychuk [PM09] solve the related polynomial moment problem through decomposition data. These results motivate the caution in Section 14.2: equality fusion of whole block spans is not a complete zero-cycle theory.

The source audit used open full texts or arXiv versions for the main theorem comparisons and authoritative bibliographic pages to check metadata and corrigenda. It was targeted, not an exhaustive MathSciNet or zbMATH citation-network review. Non-location of an equivalent formula is therefore provisional evidence of originality, not proof of priority.

16. Assurance, novelty, and remaining work

16.1 Closed in this candidate

Subject to ordinary mathematical checking of the displayed proofs, this note closes the following implications:

  • the fixed-seed rank/root-span equality has no analytic or stationary-phase hypothesis;
  • fixed sectorial rapid-decay contours give an unconditional analytic realisation, with an explicit non-singular Fourier–Gamma period matrix;
  • block fusion for every actual decomposition is exactly the Fourier rank law (8.10);
  • every block equality and pairwise intersection has the explicit criteria (8.13)–(8.16);
  • all complete decompositions and Ritt moves are classified by congruence projections of one invariant support set;
  • equality fusion exposes a canonical actual polynomial factor;
  • rank-at-most-three chains have at most two rank-creation levels and every adjacent move lies in the eleven-row Ritt table; and
  • the two faces of (x4+x)3(x^4+x)^3 are the profiles [1,1,1][1,1,1] and [3][3]; and
  • subject to the explicit computer-algebra trust boundary, the coefficient families in Theorem 13.1 exhaust q(P,dx)≤3q(P,dx)\le3 through degree twenty.

16.2 Not closed by this candidate

The following are distinct questions and are not claimed here:

  • an all-degree affine coefficient-family theorem for |Σ(P)|≤3|\Sigma(P)|\le3;
  • a classification of which abstract support subsets are polynomially realisable;
  • a new proof of Ritt’s decomposition theorems;
  • external specialist verification, formal verification, or independent reproduction;
  • settled novelty, priority, or field-wide significance; and
  • a guarantee that an arbitrarily chosen non-rapid-decay contour defines a period, or that every single rapid-decay contour is dual-cyclic.

The first two items form the remaining inverse-realisability problem. They are no longer needed to classify fusion once PP and a decomposition are given, but they are required to extend Theorem 13.1 beyond degree twenty.
