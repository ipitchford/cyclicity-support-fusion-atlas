# Abstract

Let \(P\in\mathbf C[x]\) be a polynomial phase, let \(A(x)\,dx\) be a
polynomial amplitude, and let \(G'=A\). We prove that the cyclic rank generated
by \([A\,dx]\) in the exponential twisted de Rham module is exactly the number
of non-trace residue channels in \(G(\xi(t))\), where
\(P(\xi(t))=t^{\deg P}\). Equivalently, it is the reduced constant span of the
primitive values \(G(x_j)\) on the generic inverse roots. This extends the
fixed-seed Atlas from \(dx\) to every polynomial amplitude and gives the sharp
rank-zero criterion \(G\in\mathbf C[P]\). A discrete Fourier transform then
classifies vanishing on every specified zero-dimensional cycle.

For endpoint moments

\[
\int_a^b P(x)^kA(x)\,dx=0\qquad(k\geq0),
\]

we combine the Pakovich--Muzychuk solution of the polynomial moment problem
with the all-degree right-factor audit in the preceding Atlas. Conditional
only on that still-unrefereed Atlas classification, we obtain an explicit
family-by-family answer for every phase with fixed-seed rank \(q(P)\leq3\):
powers, Dickson polynomials, quadratic- and cubic-over-power phases,
indecomposable quartics, and the exceptional degree-twelve collision.

For a Laurent phase \(L\) with pole order \(m\) at zero and an arbitrary
Laurent amplitude \(A(z)\,dz\), a local inverse
\(L(\phi(\tau))=\tau^{-m}\) gives the exact criterion

\[
\operatorname{Res}_0 L^kA\,dz
=[\tau^{mk-1}]A(\phi(\tau))\phi'(\tau).
\]

This includes logarithmic amplitudes and reduces exact forms to a class-zero
coefficient test. It does not assert a finite normal-form classification for
all Laurent phases: known indecomposable examples have persistent solutions
beyond a composition-only ansatz. Exact ordinary and optimised replays,
semantic mutations, and direct residue checks accompany the proof. The result
is an unrefereed theorem candidate, not external peer review, a complete
formalisation, or a priority claim.

# 1. Three notions of persistent vanishing

We distinguish three questions.

1. **Amplitude cyclicity.** What is the rank generated by \([A\,dx]\) under the
   exponential connection for a fixed polynomial phase \(P\)?
2. **Endpoint or zero-cycle moments.** For which amplitudes do all moments
   vanish between fixed endpoints, or on a fixed zero-dimensional cycle?
3. **Laurent residues.** For which Laurent one-forms do all residues
   \(\operatorname{Res}_0L^kA\,dz\) vanish?

These questions interact, but they are not identical. In particular,
\(q(P,dx)\leq3\) does not imply that every amplitude has cyclic rank at most
three.

Let \(d=\deg P\geq2\), put \(K=\mathbf C(s)\), and write

\[
\mathcal H_P=K[x]\,dx/(\partial_x+sP')K[x]\,dx.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The connection is

\[
\nabla_s[f\,dx]=[(\partial_sf+Pf)\,dx].
\tag{1.2}
\]

For \(A\in\mathbf C[x]\), define

\[
q(P,A\,dx)=\dim_K K\langle\nabla_s\rangle[A\,dx].
\tag{1.3}
\]

An affine source change transports both \(P\) and \(A\,dx\); an affine target
change acts triangularly on powers of \(P\). We may take \(P\) monic and
centred when computing support.

# 2. Primitive-amplitude support

Let

\[
P(\xi(t))=t^d,\qquad \xi(t)=t+O(t^{-1})
\tag{2.1}
\]

be the normalised inverse at infinity, and let \(G'=A\). Write

\[
G(\xi(t))=\sum_{n\leq N}c_nt^n.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The **primitive-amplitude support** is

\[
\Sigma(P;G)=
\{r\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}:c_n
\text{ is nonzero for some }n\equiv r\pmod d\}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Changing the additive constant in \(G\) changes only the trace channel.

## Theorem 2.1 — Polynomial-amplitude support theorem

For every polynomial phase and polynomial amplitude,

\[
\boxed{
q(P,A\,dx)=|\Sigma(P;G)|
=\dim_{\mathbf C}\operatorname{span}^{\mathrm{red}}
\{G(x_0),\ldots,G(x_{d-1})\}.
}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Here \(x_j=\xi(\zeta_d^jt)\), and “red” removes the common trace component.

### Proof

The formal moment isomorphism of [EP3] is reproduced in the form needed here.
Pull a one-form back by \(x=\xi(t)\). In nonzero residue class \(r\pmod d\),
send

\[
t^{r+nd-1}dt\longmapsto
\mu_{r,n}s^{-n}e_r,\qquad
\mu_{r,n+1}=-\frac{r+nd}{d}\mu_{r,n}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

All \(\mu_{r,n}\) are nonzero. Equation \(P(\xi)=t^d\) makes
\((\partial_x+sP')h\,dx\) pull back to
\((\partial_t+sd t^{d-1})h(\xi(t))\,dt\), which maps to zero by (2.5).
The map descends to

\[
\mathcal H_P\otimes_K\mathbf C((s))
\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_{r=1}^{d-1}\mathbf C((s))e_r.
\tag{2.6}
\]

On \(dx,x\,dx,\ldots,x^{d-2}dx\), its constant matrix is triangular with
diagonal one because \(\xi^j\xi'=t^j+O(t^{j-2})\). Hence (2.6) is an
isomorphism.

Now

\[
A(\xi)\xi' dt=d(G(\xi))=\sum_n n c_nt^{n-1}dt.
\tag{2.7}
\]

For a nonzero residue \(r\), \(n\) cannot vanish when
\(n\equiv r\pmod d\). Distinct
coefficients occupy distinct powers of \(s\), so the \(r\)-th component is
nonzero exactly when \(r\in\Sigma(P;G)\). Active Kummer components have
distinct fractional exponents; their derivative Wronskian is a nonzero
Vandermonde determinant [EP3, Lemma 5.1]. Their cyclic rank is their number.

Finally,
\[
Y_r(t)=\frac1d\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\zeta_d^{-jr}G(x_j(t))
\]
is the part of (2.2) in residue class \(r\). Fourier inversion and disjoint
Laurent supports prove the reduced-span equality. \(\square\)

## Corollary 2.2 — Universal rank-zero amplitudes

\[
q(P,A\,dx)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Sigma(P;G)=\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
G\in\mathbf C[P].
\tag{2.8}
\]

Equivalently, \(A(x)=P'(x)H'(P(x))\) for some polynomial \(H\).

### Proof

If \(G=H\circ P\), then \(G(\xi(t))=H(t^d)\). Conversely, empty non-trace
support makes \(G(x_j)\) equal on all generic inverse roots. Hence
\(G\in\mathbf C(P)\) inside \(\mathbf C(x)\). Because both \(P\) and \(G\)
are polynomials and have no finite pole,
\(\mathbf C(P)\cap\mathbf C[x]=\mathbf C[P]\). \(\square\)

## Example 2.3 — Low phase rank does not bound amplitude rank

For \(P=(x^4+x)^3\), \(q(P,dx)=3\). Taking \(G=x^4\), \(A=4x^3\), gives

\[
\Sigma(P;x^4)=\{1,4,7,10\},\qquad q(P,4x^3dx)=4.
\tag{2.9}
\]

# 3. Exact zero-cycle classification

Let \(n=(n_0,\ldots,n_{d-1})\in\mathbf C^d\), \(\sum_jn_j=0\), and put

\[
\widehat n(r)=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}n_j\zeta_d^{jr}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

## Theorem 3.1 — Support annihilation on a zero-cycle

\[
\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}n_jG(x_j(t))\equiv0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\widehat n(r)=0
\text{ for every }r\in\Sigma(P;G).
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof

Fourier inversion writes \(G(x_j)=\sum_r\zeta_d^{jr}Y_r\). The trace term
vanishes because \(\widehat n(0)=0\). Nonzero \(Y_r\) are constant-linearly
independent because their exponents occupy disjoint residue classes. \(\square\)

This is complete for a specified phase, amplitude, and cycle. It does not make
the broader balanced-cycle literature composition-only [ABM13].

For \(P=(x^4+x)^3\), the cycle \(n_j=\cos(2\pi j/3)\) has Fourier support
\(\{4,8\}\). The primitives

\[
G_7=x^7+\frac74x^4,\qquad G_{10}=x^{10}-\frac52x^4
\tag{3.3}
\]

both have support \(\{1,7,10\}\), proving their zero-cycle vanishing. Their
amplitudes are \(7x^6+7x^3\) and \(10x^9-10x^3\).

# 4. Endpoint moments and the imported theorem

Fix \(a,b\in\mathbf C\), put \(G'=A\), and define

\[
M_k(P,A;a,b)=\int_a^bP(x)^kA(x)\,dx.
\tag{4.1}
\]

## Theorem 4.1 — Polynomial moment theorem [PM09]

All \(M_k(P,A;a,b)\) vanish for \(k\geq0\) if and only if

\[
G=\sum_{j=1}^r H_j\circ W_j,
\tag{4.2}
\]

where \(P=R_j\circ W_j\) and \(W_j(a)=W_j(b)\) for every \(j\).

Necessity is imported from [PM09], not reproved. Sufficiency follows by
substitution \(u=W_j(x)\). Pakovich's refinement [P13] describes the
indecomposable right factors explicitly.

If \(P(a)\) and \(P(b)\) differ, no nonconstant right factor is
endpoint-admissible, so
the only solution is \(A=0\). Hence assume \(P(a)=P(b)\).

# 5. Persistent endpoint moments for every \(q(P)\leq3\)

This section specialises Theorem 4.1 using the all-degree normal forms and
right-factor audit of [EP3]. Because [EP3] is unrefereed relative to named
monodromy classifications, the same qualification attaches here. Formulas are
in Atlas coordinates; affine source changes transport \(a,b,A\,dx,G\).

## Theorem 5.1 — Low-rank persistent-moment classification

Let \(q(P,dx)\leq3\), let \(a,b\) be distinct, and let \(P(a)=P(b)\).
All endpoint moments
vanish if and only if \(G\) belongs to the final-column space below.

| Phase | Endpoint-admissible factors | Complete space for \(G\) |
|---|---|---|
| \(P=x^d\) | \(x^h\), where \(h\) is the order of \(a/b\) | \(\mathbf C[x^h]\) |
| \(P=D_d(x,\alpha)\) | divisibility-minimal \(D_e(x,\alpha)\) with \(e\mid d\) and \(D_e(a,\alpha)=D_e(b,\alpha)\) | \(\sum_{e\in E_{\min}}\mathbf C[D_e]\) |
| \(P=H(x^m)\), \(\deg H=2\) | least \(x^h\), \(h\mid m\), with \(a^h=b^h\), if one exists; otherwise \(P\) | \(\mathbf C[x^h]\), or \(\mathbf C[P]\) |
| \(P=H(x^m)\), \(\deg H=3\), off the aligned-square locus | the same power factor, if it exists; otherwise \(P\) | \(\mathbf C[x^h]\), or \(\mathbf C[P]\) |
| \(P=H(x^{2k})=(x^{3k}+b x^k)^2+C\) | \(P\), admissible power factors, and \(F=x^{3k}+b x^k\) when \(F(a)=F(b)\) | \(\mathbf C[P]+\mathbf 1_{F(a)=F(b)}\mathbf C[F]+\mathbf 1_{a^h=b^h}\mathbf C[x^h]\) |
| indecomposable \(P=x^4+\alpha x^2+\beta x\), with \(\beta\) nonzero | \(P\) only | \(\mathbf C[P]\) |
| \(P=(x^4+\alpha x)^3\), with \(\alpha\) nonzero | \(P\), \(W_4=x^4+\alpha x\), and \(W_3=x^3\), subject to endpoint equality | \(\mathbf C[P]+\mathbf 1_{W_4(a)=W_4(b)}\mathbf C[W_4]+\mathbf 1_{a^3=b^3}\mathbf C[x^3]\) |

Here \(E_{\min}\) consists of divisibility-minimal elements of
\[
E=\{e:e\mid d,\ D_e(a,\alpha)=D_e(b,\alpha)\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
In over-power rows, omit the \(h\)-term if no divisor of \(m\) has equal
endpoint values. Lower-rank overlaps use the earliest applicable row.

### Proof

Theorem 4.1 says that the solution space is the sum of \(\mathbf C[W]\) over
endpoint-admissible right factors. For \(x^d\), all factors are \(x^e\),
\(e\mid d\); endpoint equality holds exactly when \(h\mid e\), and their
algebras lie in \(\mathbf C[x^h]\).

The Dickson identity
\[
D_d(x,\alpha)=D_{d/e}(D_e(x,\alpha),\alpha^e)
\tag{5.2}
\]
gives its factors. If \(e_1\mid e_2\), then
\(\mathbf C[D_{e_2}]\subseteq\mathbf C[D_{e_1}]\); only minimal admissible
factors are needed, while incomparable factors must be summed.

For \(H(x^m)\), ordinary suffixes are \(x^e\), \(e\mid m\), and collapse to
the least admissible \(x^h\). If none is admissible, \(P\) remains. The sole
additional rank-three suffix is
\[
H(x^{2k})-C=(x^{3k}+b x^k)^2.
\tag{5.3}
\]
It contributes \(\mathbf C[F]\) exactly when \(F(a)=F(b)\).

An indecomposable quartic has no proper nonlinear factor. The exceptional
collision has the incomparable factors \(x^4+\alpha x\) and \(x^3\), since
\[
(x^4+\alpha x)^3=x^3(x^3+\alpha)^3=\widetilde H(x^3).
\tag{5.4}
\]
The table follows from Theorem 4.1. \(\square\)

## Corollary 5.2 — Decision procedure

Affine-normalise \(P\), locate its Atlas row, evaluate the listed factors at
\(a,b\), and test whether \(G\) belongs to the resulting sum of polynomial
subalgebras. This is exact decomposition, not moment sampling.

# 6. Laurent phases and arbitrary Laurent amplitudes

Let
\[
L(z)=\sum_{i=-m}^{n}c_iz^i,\qquad m,n\geq1,
\tag{6.1}
\]
where \(c_{-m}\) and \(c_n\) are nonzero, and let \(A(z)\,dz\) be any
Laurent-polynomial one-form. Divide \(L\) by
\(c_{-m}\). There is a unique formal series
\(\phi(\tau)=\tau(1+O(\tau))\) satisfying
\[
L(\phi(\tau))=\tau^{-m}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

## Theorem 6.1 — Laurent local-channel criterion

For every \(k\geq1\),
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}L(z)^kA(z)\,dz
=[\tau^{mk-1}]A(\phi(\tau))\phi'(\tau).
}
\tag{6.3}
\]
Thus all Laurent moments vanish if and only if all displayed coefficients
vanish.

### Proof

Formal residues are invariant under \(z=\phi(\tau)\). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}L^kA\,dz
=\operatorname{Res}_{\tau=0}
\tau^{-mk}A(\phi(\tau))\phi'(\tau)d\tau,
\]
whose \(\tau^{-1}\) coefficient is (6.3). \(\square\)

This includes the logarithmic class \(z^{-1}dz\).

## Corollary 6.2 — Exact Laurent amplitudes

If \(A=G'\) for Laurent polynomial \(G\), then
\[
\operatorname{Res}_{0}L^k\,dG
=mk[\tau^{mk}]G(\phi(\tau)).
\tag{6.4}
\]

If \(G=H\circ L\), this vanishes because \(G(\phi)=H(\tau^{-m})\). A second
mechanism is deck symmetry: if \(L=R(z^e)\), excluded residue classes vanish
under \(z\mapsto\zeta_ez\). For example,
\(\operatorname{Res}_0(z^2+z^{-2})^kdz=0\) for every \(k\). This corrects the
inherited exploratory notebook's undercount for symmetric phases.

# 7. Why the Laurent result stops here

Theorem 6.1 is exact for specified \(L,A\), but it is not a finite
classification of all pairs. Pakovich, Pech, and Zvonkin [PPZ10] exhibit an
indecomposable Laurent phase whose persistent solution space is a free module
of rank five over \(\mathbf C[L]\). A simple composition-only description is
therefore insufficient.

This release does not infer all-\(k\) vanishing from a finite zero prefix,
promote inherited small-bidegree tables to a theorem, or claim a finite
all-Laurent analogue of Theorem 5.1. It supplies the exact coefficient object
against which future proposals must be proved.

# 8. Exact computational checks

The implementation compares Krylov reduction of \(A,PA,P^2A,\ldots\) with
coefficient extraction from \(G(\xi(t))\). It stops only when the witnessed
support size equals the independently computed cyclic rank. Controls include
powers, Dickson phases, affine transport, the rank-four amplitude, and two
exceptional zero-cycle centres.

For Laurent inputs, local inverse coefficients are compared with direct
Laurent multiplication and residues at every tested moment. Controls include
a logarithmic amplitude, deck symmetry, nonvanishing, target scaling, and
exact forms.

Ordinary and optimised executions produce byte-identical receipts. Two
semantic mutations are rejected after digest recomputation. Sixteen endpoint
moments and finite Laurent prefixes remain regressions, not infinite proofs.

# 9. Assurance boundary and open problems

This manuscript proves the polynomial-amplitude support formula, rank-zero
criterion, specified zero-cycle criterion, and arbitrary-Laurent-form local
criterion. The endpoint table additionally imports [PM09] and the unrefereed
Atlas factor classification [EP3]. It is a complete specialisation in that
framework, not an independent reproof of monodromy or Ritt classification.

Next priorities are external specialist review; formalisation of the moment
isomorphism; finite structural conditions equivalent to the Laurent test for
controlled phases; and classification of amplitudes of bounded induced rank.
