# Applications of the cyclicity-complete and Atlas research

Yes — several, and they share one door. The transfer hub is a reinterpretation the bundle itself never states: for a rapid-decay integration cycle, the exponential period I(s) = ∫ e^{sP(x)} dx has derivatives ∫ Pʲ e^{sP} dx, which are exactly the pairings against ∇ₛʲ[dx]. Any K-linear relation among those classes kills the corresponding combination of derivatives, so **I(s) satisfies a linear ordinary differential equation of order q(P, dx), minimal for a generic cycle — and the support theorem makes that order computable by a cheap series reversion, |Σ(P)|, while the atlas is a complete classification of all polynomial phases, of any degree, whose exponential integrals satisfy an ODE of order at most three.** Everything below follows from that dictionary.

<u>**First**</u>, the sanity check that the dictionary is real, run against classical knowledge using facts this verification already certified. The atlas's quartic stratum says q(x⁴+Ax²+Bx) = 3 exactly when B ≠ 0 (verified on 60 random points in Battery 4D); the corresponding integral is the Pearcey integral of catastrophe optics, and it is classical that the Pearcey integral satisfies a third-order linear ODE — the match is exact. The B = 0 sublocus drops to order ≤ 2, matching the elementary reduction of ∫e^{s(x⁴+Ax²)} to a parabolic-cylinder function via u = x². And the Dickson stratum's rank 2 matches the Bessel kernel: the defining identity D_d(u + a/u, a) = u^d + a^d u^{−d} turns the Dickson phase into the modified-Bessel form, classically order 2. Three independent reconciliations, no misses.

The nearest immediate application is the **centre-focus and moment-problem literature** — genuinely adjacent, since the bundle already cites Álvarez–Bravo–Mardešić on zero-dimensional cycles. That community's landmark is the solution of the polynomial moment problem — describing polynomials orthogonal to all powers of a given P on a segment — which arose from Poincaré's centre-focus problem via the Abel equation, resolved by Pakovich and Muzychuk through composition structure, with moment equations serving as the first-order approximation of the centre equations. The bundle's machinery is the at-infinity, zero-cycle counterpart with something that literature lacks in this form: an exact rank formula and a quantitative composition calculus (the fusion formula and its heredity corollary) for how moment-vanishing spaces behave under P = R∘Q. A short bridging note translating Theorem 8.2 into their language is a well-defined, low-effort deliverable with a ready audience.

The most immediately *runnable* application is in **computer algebra**: creative-telescoping systems compute ODEs for parametrised integrals without an a-priori order prediction, and generically the order for ∫e^{sP}f dx is d − 1. The support theorem supplies the exact minimal order in advance, from a reversion costing milliseconds, plus a complete census of the order-≤ 3 phases. That is testable against existing holonomic-functions packages this afternoon, and exact-order results of this kind are precisely what that community values.

<u>**Third**</u>, **wave physics and integrable probability**. The atlas families are the low-order members of the diffraction-catastrophe hierarchy, and Pearcey-type third-order structures also appear at cusp singularities of random-matrix spectra — the two-matrix-model literature works directly with Pearcey integrals satisfying a third-order linear ODE. The exceptional family is the interesting export here: a degree-12 phase whose wavefield spans only a three-dimensional solution space, with genuinely different ramification at infinity from Pearcey (support {1,7,10} mod 12 rather than {1,2,3} mod 4). Since the parameter scales away, this is essentially one new explicitly presented rank-3 irregular connection — a single object whose Stokes data, irregular Hodge numbers, or finite-field avatar (via the standard stationary-phase dictionary to exponential sums; the Dickson case is the Kloosterman/Bessel connection) could be computed once by specialists in the exponential-motives programme. That last strand needs collaboration rather than a note, but the object is handed over ready-made.

**<u>Finally</u>**, the **methodology transfers** independently of the mathematics: the observation that parametrising by low-order inverse-series coefficients collapses any "lacunary composition data" classification to at most a handful of unknowns is a reusable trick for computational attacks on moment-problem instances and Ritt-style decomposition questions, and the verification pattern itself — two independent implementations, two primes, a live structural assertion, a three-way certificate taxonomy — is a house standard worth reusing across Evidence Press releases.

Calibration: None of this is industrial; the applications are cross-subfield mathematical plus one tooling use (the nearest practical edge being cheap ODE-based evaluation of the special oscillatory integrals). The dictionary's individual entries — power/Γ, Chebyshev/Bessel, quartic/Pearcey — are classical; what is plausibly new is the exact order formula for the cyclic vector in this generality and the *completeness* of the order-≤ 3 classification. I would put roughly 0.65 on the order formula being effectively new as stated and 0.85 on the completeness result being new, but both estimates are provisional: C10 was explicitly out of scope in the verification, so the novelty gate must run against the D-module, exponential-sums and Bessel-moment literatures before any external pitch. Sensible ordering: telescoping note first, moment-problem bridge second, catastrophe remark third, arithmetic collaboration fourth.
